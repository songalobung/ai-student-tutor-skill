# Evaluation Criteria: Assessment Reliability & Marking Fairness

This document specifies evaluation criteria for the tutor's assessment operations, ensuring that questions are psychometrically sound, grading is fair and repeatable, and feedback is transparently tied to mark allocation.

---

## 1. Assessment Validity & Reliability Standards

1. **Construct Validity**: Questions must assess the targeted curriculum objective rather than secondary non-construct hurdles (e.g., a science question should not become a test of complex linguistic parsing).
2. **Mark Scheme Fidelity**: When evaluating student responses, the tutor must strictly follow the published or generated marking breakdown ($M, A, B$ marks).
3. **Consistency & Repeatability**: Given identical student submissions, the tutor must award consistent marks across repeated trials ($\kappa \ge 0.90$).
4. **Follow-Through ($FT$) Fairness**: If a student makes an early calculation error, verify that subsequent method steps carrying that error forward are credited appropriately.

---

## 2. Evaluation Test Scenarios

### Scenario A: Mathematical Arithmetic Error with Correct Method
- **Input**: Student solves a 3-step calculus optimization problem. Step 1 derivative is correct ($M1$). Step 2 setting derivative to zero contains an arithmetic slip ($A0$). Step 3 correctly identifies the sign of the second derivative using their flawed critical value.
- **Evaluation Criteria**: Tutor must award $M1, A0, M1(FT)$. The tutor must clearly explain that the method in Step 3 was sound, preventing the student from wrongly doubting their grasp of the optimization concept.

### Scenario B: Unsubstantiated Essay Assertion
- **Input**: In an Economics 12-mark evaluation prompt, the student writes: *"Monopoly is bad because it raises prices."*
- **Evaluation Criteria**: The tutor must not award Level 3/4 evaluation marks for bare assertions. It must highlight the need for economic mechanisms (e.g., deadweight welfare loss, allocative inefficiency, barriers to entry, abnormal profits) and prompt for balanced counter-arguments (e.g., economies of scale, dynamic efficiency, natural monopolies).
