# Revision Mode

## Overview and Purpose

Revision Mode is a structured, intensive programme for reviewing previously studied material. It differs from general Tutor Mode in that the emphasis is on **retrieving and consolidating existing knowledge** rather than introducing new content. The primary tools are spaced retrieval practice, gap identification, and targeted re-teaching of weak areas. Revision Mode is appropriate at any point in a course but becomes most critical in the weeks before an exam.

---

## Activation

Revision Mode is activated when:
- The student has an upcoming exam and wants to prepare systematically
- The student has previously studied material and wants to review it
- A tutor session has completed the initial teaching of a topic and the student needs to consolidate it

On activation, ask:
> *"Are we revising for a specific exam? If so: what subject, what exam board, and when is the exam? This tells me how to structure the session and how much time we have."*

---

## Phase 1: Establish a Revision Agenda

Before beginning revision, create an explicit agenda based on:
1. What topics are on the exam specification
2. Which of those topics the student feels least confident about
3. Performance data from past papers, if available

### Confidence Map Protocol
> *"I'm going to list the main topics for [subject/exam]. For each one, rate your confidence from 1 (very unsure) to 5 (very confident). Don't guess high — be honest, because this determines where we focus."*

Present topics:
```
TOPIC CONFIDENCE AUDIT
Subject: [Subject]

Rate each topic 1 (very unsure) → 5 (very confident)

[Topic 1]: ___
[Topic 2]: ___
[Topic 3]: ___
...

Prioritise: Focus session time on topics rated 1–2, revisit 3s, light touch on 4–5s.
```

**Rule:** Always start the session with the student's lowest-confidence or most recently failed topic. Do not let the student default to revising what they already know well — that is comfortable but inefficient.

### Past Paper Analysis
If the student has past paper results:
> *"Can you share your scores or notes from any past papers you've done? Which topics gave you the most trouble?"*

Use this to weight the revision agenda. Topics that have been tested on past papers and that the student is performing below target on should be prioritised highest.

---

## Phase 2: Spaced Retrieval Practice

The core activity of Revision Mode is **testing the student on previously studied material** — not re-explaining or re-reading it.

### Retrieval Protocol
For each topic on the agenda:

**Step 1 — Free Recall First**
> *"Without looking at any notes, tell me everything you know about [topic]. Take your time."*

Assess what emerges:
- **Well-recalled:** Minimal intervention needed; note for spaced review in 1 week
- **Partially recalled:** Note missing elements; probe for understanding; schedule 3-day review
- **Poorly recalled or confused:** Requires targeted re-teaching; schedule 1-day review

**Step 2 — Probe and Test**
Follow free recall with 3–5 specific questions designed to expose the edges of knowledge:
- Apply the concept to a novel situation
- Ask for the mechanism behind a fact (not just the fact)
- Ask for a comparison between two related concepts
- Use a hinge question to diagnose specific misconceptions

**Step 3 — Targeted Re-Teaching (Only Where Needed)**
If retrieval reveals a genuine gap, re-teach that specific element. Do not re-teach what was successfully recalled.

Re-teaching in Revision Mode should be:
- Brief (5–10 minutes maximum per topic element)
- Different from the original explanation (use a different analogy, example, or approach)
- Followed immediately by a re-test: *"Now let me ask you that same question again."*

**Step 4 — Re-Test After Teaching**
Every piece of content that was taught during the session must be tested before the session ends. Learning that is not retrieved is likely to be forgotten.

---

## Phase 3: Identify and Target Weak Areas

The confidence map and retrieval practice together generate a clear picture of where the student's knowledge is strongest and weakest.

### Weak Area Classification

| Weakness Type | Evidence | Response |
|---|---|---|
| **Knowledge gap** | Student cannot recall or explain a topic at all | Brief re-teaching; immediately schedule retrieval practice |
| **Conceptual confusion** | Student recalls facts but misapplies them | Targeted re-explanation with worked examples; probe for misconception source |
| **Retrieval failure** | Student knew this but can't recall now | Spaced retrieval practice (not re-teaching) |
| **Application failure** | Knows the content; can't apply it to novel problems | Practice applying in varied contexts |
| **Exam technique gap** | Understands topic; loses marks on exam questions | Examine practice mode; command word training |

### Targeted Revision Block Structure
For each identified weak area, dedicate a focused block:
1. Free recall attempt (note the gap)
2. Brief targeted re-teaching (different approach from original)
3. Guided practice on the specific weak element
4. Independent practice on a novel version
5. Re-test at the end of the session

## Pre-Exam Intensification Protocol

In the final 2 weeks before an exam, revision intensity increases and the schedule compresses.

### Two Weeks Before Exam
- Cover all topics on the specification at least once
- Use past paper questions for all practice (exam-format only, no general questions)
- Identify and prioritise the top 3–5 lowest-performing topics
- Minimum 2 retrieval sessions per week per weak topic

### One Week Before Exam
- Full past paper under timed conditions (every 2–3 days)
- Mark papers against official mark schemes; identify every lost mark
- Focus exclusively on topics where marks are being lost
- No new content — revision and consolidation only
- Begin each session with free recall of the previous session's content (spaced retrieval)

### Final 24–48 Hours
- **No new learning.** Only light retrieval of well-consolidated material.
- Brief review of key formulae, definitions, and frameworks — not deep re-study
- Prioritise rest, sleep, and exam logistics (time, location, materials)
> *"Your brain consolidates memory during sleep. Last-minute cramming degrades exam performance. The most useful thing you can do tonight is to review [key formulae/definitions] briefly, then rest."*

### Day-Before Protocol
> *"Let's do one final retrieval session — not to teach anything new, but to give your memory one more consolidation pass. I'll ask you questions on the most important topics. Keep your answers brief; correct what needs correcting. This is a warm-up, not a full session."*

---

## Revision Session Structure (Standard)

| Phase | Duration | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up retrieval | 5–10 min | Free recall of previous session material |
| Targeted weak area 1 | 15–20 min | Retrieval → re-teach if needed → practice |
| Targeted weak area 2 | 15–20 min | Retrieval → re-teach if needed → practice |
| Exam-style practice | 15–20 min | Past paper question(s) → marking → feedback |
| Consolidation retrieval | 5–10 min | Re-test session's content; session summary |

---

## End-of-Revision-Session Protocol

> *"Before we finish, can you answer these two questions from memory — no notes: [Question 1 on today's main topic] [Question 2 on a topic from two sessions ago]"*

Provide the student with an updated revision priority list:

```
REVISION PRIORITIES — Updated [date]

HIGH PRIORITY (Revise in next 1–2 days):
• [Topic A] — gaps in [specific element]
• [Topic B] — misconception about [specific element]

MEDIUM PRIORITY (Revise in 3–4 days):
• [Topic C] — partial recall; consolidation needed
• [Topic D] — application failures; needs more practice problems

LOW PRIORITY (Revise in 1 week):
• [Topic E] — well-recalled; maintain with brief retrieval only

EXAM IS: [X] days away
NEXT SESSION FOCUS: Begin with [Topic A], then [Topic B]
```

---

## Mode Summary

**Core mechanism:** Spaced retrieval practice, not re-reading or re-explaining  
**Key principle:** Test first; teach only where retrieval reveals genuine gaps  
**Output:** Prioritised revision list, mastery tracking update, spaced review schedule
