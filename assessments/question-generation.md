# Question Generation & Test Item Design

This document details the generation protocols for creating academically rigorous, syllabus-aligned, and cognitively diverse assessment items across all student proficiency tiers.

---

## 1. Cognitive Depth Calibration (Bloom's Revised Taxonomy)

When creating question sets or test batteries, generate items across six explicit cognitive dimensions:

| Level | Cognitive Objective | Target Command Words | Example Task Specification |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Remember** | Recall factual data, terms, formulas, and baseline definitions. | State, Define, List, Identify | "Define the term *activation energy*." |
| **2. Understand** | Demonstrate comprehension by translating, interpreting, or summarizing. | Describe, Explain, Clarify, Contrast | "Explain why sound waves cannot propagate through a vacuum." |
| **3. Apply** | Execute procedures or apply formulas in novel numerical/situational contexts. | Calculate, Determine, Solve, Show that | "Calculate the standard enthalpy change of combustion of ethanol given bond energies." |
| **4. Analyse** | Break information into component parts, deduce cause-and-effect, resolve ambiguities. | Deduce, Compare, Distinguish, Dissect | "Analyse the structural factors that make diamond extremely hard while graphite acts as a lubricant." |
| **5. Evaluate** | Make reasoned judgments based on qualitative criteria, data, or opposing arguments. | Assess, Evaluate, Justify, Appraise | "Evaluate whether monetary policy is more effective than fiscal policy in curbing demand-pull inflation." |
| **6. Create** | Formulate new hypotheses, design experiments, or synthesize interdisciplinary solutions. | Design, Formulate, Synthesize, Devise | "Design a laboratory procedure using a simple pendulum to determine local gravitational acceleration $g$." |

---

## 2. Item Generation Principles

1. **Unambiguous Prompting**: The student must know precisely what type of response is expected, the depth of reasoning required, and the target mark value.
2. **Context Authenticity**: Avoid purely mechanical and artificial phrasing. Use realistic empirical scenarios, real physical constants, and plausible financial/scientific data.
3. **Plausible Distractors for Multiple Choice**:
   - Every incorrect option in an MCQ must correspond to a known, verified student misconception (e.g., forgetting to invert fractions when dividing, forgetting to convert $\text{km/h}$ to $\text{m/s}$, confusing supply shifts with movements along the curve).
   - Never use joke options, obviously nonsensical choices, or "all of the above" unless pedagogical diagnosis specifically calls for compound evaluations.
4. **Mark Allocation Transparency**: Always state explicit mark values (e.g., `[3 marks]`) next to questions to signal the expected number of distinct valid points or steps:
   - 1 Mark: Direct factual statement, unit, or single numerical value.
   - 2 Marks: Definition plus qualifying clause; or standard 1-step calculation showing formula and answer.
   - 3–4 Marks: Multi-step calculation with intermediate substitutions; or mechanism explanation (cause $\to$ effect $\to$ outcome).
   - 6+ Marks: Structured comparative essay, multi-variable proof, or policy evaluation.

---

## 3. Tiered Item Progression (The Scaffolded Problem Set)

When generating a practice sequence within a lesson, always structure questions in a 3-tier cascade:

```
[Tier 1: Routine / Drill] (Direct application of formula; standard familiar framing)
          |
          v
[Tier 2: Intermediate / Non-Standard] (Multi-step calculation; unit conversion; hidden variable)
          |
          v
[Tier 3: Synoptic / Exam-Style] (Unfamiliar context; multiple concepts combined; qualitative evaluation)
```

### Example: Tiered Cascade for Projectile Motion
- **Tier 1 (2 marks)**: "A ball is launched horizontally at $15\text{ m/s}$ from a cliff $20\text{ m}$ high. Calculate the time taken to hit the ground."
- **Tier 2 (4 marks)**: "A ball is kicked from ground level with velocity $25\text{ m/s}$ at an angle of $35^\circ$ above the horizontal. Calculate the horizontal range and maximum height achieved."
- **Tier 3 (6 marks)**: "An artillery shell is fired toward a target located on a hillside that slopes upward at an angle of $10^\circ$. If the initial velocity is $u$ at angle $\theta$, deduce an expression for the distance up the incline where the shell impacts. Discuss how atmospheric drag would alter your trajectory."
