# Student Misconception Knowledge Base & Remediation Catalog

Rather than merely instructing the tutor to "detect errors," this knowledge base catalogs the most frequent, persistent misconceptions across core academic disciplines, detailing their diagnostic symptoms, underlying cognitive causes, and verified pedagogical remediation protocols.

---

## 1. Mathematics Misconceptions

### 1.1 Misconception M-01: Linearity of Non-Linear Operators ("The Freshman's Dream")
- **Manifestation**: Student writes $(a + b)^2 = a^2 + b^2$, $\sqrt{a + b} = \sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b}$, or $\sin(x + y) = \sin(x) + \sin(y)$.
- **Cognitive Cause**: Overgeneralization of the distributive property of multiplication over addition $c(a + b) = ca + cb$ to non-linear functions and powers.
- **Diagnostic Question**: *"Evaluate $(3 + 4)^2$ by adding inside first, then evaluate $3^2 + 4^2$. Do they equal each other?"*
- **Remediation Protocol**:
  1. Have the student compute with concrete numbers ($7^2 = 49$ vs. $9 + 16 = 25$).
  2. Draw a geometric area diagram of a square with sides $(a + b)$, illustrating the two missing rectangles of area $ab$ (total area $a^2 + 2ab + b^2$).
  3. Enforce the rule: exponents apply to factors, not individual terms inside a sum.

### 1.2 Misconception M-02: Universal Cancellation in Fractions
- **Manifestation**: In $\frac{x + 5}{x}$, student cancels $x$ to get $1 + 5 = 6$ or $5$.
- **Cognitive Cause**: Failing to distinguish between additive terms and multiplicative factors in algebraic fractions.
- **Diagnostic Question**: *"Substitute $x = 10$ into $\frac{x + 5}{x}$. What is $\frac{15}{10}$? Does that equal $5$ or $6$?"*
- **Remediation Protocol**:
  1. Contrast $\frac{5 \cdot x}{x} = 5$ (valid cancellation of common factor) with $\frac{5 + x}{x}$ (invalid cancellation across addition).
  2. Explain that division distributes across addition: $\frac{x + 5}{x} = \frac{x}{x} + \frac{5}{x} = 1 + \frac{5}{x}$.

## 2. Physics Misconceptions

### 2.1 Misconception P-01: Motion Requires Continuous Force ("The Aristotelian Intuition")
- **Manifestation**: Student believes that if an object is moving at constant velocity, there must be a constant net force pushing it in the direction of motion.
- **Cognitive Cause**: Everyday friction-dominated intuition where carts stop unless pulled continuously.
- **Diagnostic Question**: *"A puck slides across frictionless ice at $5\text{ m/s}$. What horizontal force is required to keep it moving at that constant speed?"*
- **Remediation Protocol**:
  1. Re-anchor Newton's First Law: Net force causes *acceleration* (change in velocity), not velocity itself.
  2. Guide the student through a thought experiment: Imagine reducing friction from rough carpet $\to$ smooth wood $\to$ polished ice $\to$ deep space vacuum.
  3. Contrast $F_{\text{net}} = ma$ where $a = 0 \implies F_{\text{net}} = 0$.

### 2.2 Misconception P-02: Conflating Mass and Weight
- **Manifestation**: Using kilograms as a unit of force, or believing an object becomes weightless when gravity drops to zero without distinguishing between inertial mass ($m$) and gravitational force ($W = mg$).
- **Cognitive Cause**: Everyday colloquial language where people say "I weigh 70 kg."
- **Remediation Protocol**:
  1. Contrast inertial mass (resistance to acceleration, measured in $\text{kg}$, constant anywhere in the universe) with weight (gravitational pull, measured in Newtons $\text{N}$, depends on local $g$).
  2. Ask student to calculate their weight on the Moon ($g = 1.6\text{ N/kg}$) vs. Earth ($g = 9.8\text{ N/kg}$).

## 3. Chemistry Misconceptions

### 3.1 Misconception C-01: Molecules of Ionic Compounds
- **Manifestation**: Referring to a "molecule of sodium chloride" or believing ionic bonding consists of distinct, isolated $\text{NaCl}$ pairs.
- **Cognitive Cause**: Overgeneralization of covalent molecular models to all chemical compounds.
- **Remediation Protocol**:
  1. Introduce the **Giant Ionic Lattice**: Explain that sodium chloride consists of a repeating 3D lattice where each $\text{Na}^+$ ion is surrounded electrostatically by $6\text{ Cl}^-$ ions.
  2. Clarify that the formula $\text{NaCl}$ is an empirical formula representing the simplest whole-number ratio, not a discrete molecule.

### 3.2 Misconception C-02: Equilibrium Stops Reactions
- **Manifestation**: Believing that once dynamic equilibrium is reached, all chemical reactions stop completely.
- **Remediation Protocol**:
  1. Stress the word **Dynamic**: The forward reaction and reverse reaction continue simultaneously at identical rates ($\text{rate}_{\text{forward}} = \text{rate}_{\text{reverse}}$).
  2. Use a bank account analogy: If you deposit \$10 per minute and your friend withdraws \$10 per minute, the balance stays constant, but money is actively flowing in both directions.

## 4. Biology Misconceptions

### 4.1 Misconception B-01: Lamarckian Inheritance ("Acquired Traits are Inherited")
- **Manifestation**: Believing giraffes grew long necks because ancestral giraffes stretched their necks to reach tall trees, passing that stretched trait to offspring.
- **Cognitive Cause**: Teleological intuition that organisms evolve because they "need" or "want" to adapt.
- **Remediation Protocol**:
  1. Present the Darwinian selection mechanism: Random genetic variation/mutation existed in ancestral populations.
  2. Environmental selection pressure: Individuals possessing alleles for longer necks had a survival/reproductive advantage during droughts.
  3. Differential reproduction over generations shifted allele frequencies across the population.

## 5. Economics Misconceptions

### 5.1 Misconception E-01: Movement Along Curve vs. Shift of Curve
- **Manifestation**: Student claims that an increase in price causes the demand curve to shift left.
- **Cognitive Cause**: Conflating a change in **Quantity Demanded** ($\Delta Q_D$, movement along the fixed curve caused by price) with a change in **Demand** (shift of the entire curve caused by non-price factors like income, tastes, or substitutes).
- **Remediation Protocol**:
  1. Present the formal rule: If the variable is on the graph axis (Price), it causes a **movement along the curve**.
  2. If the variable is an external non-price determinant, it causes a **shift of the entire curve**.
